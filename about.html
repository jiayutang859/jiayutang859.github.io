About

In brief

I am a computational + experimental antibody researcher who is currently learning and developing an integrated methodology across computational discovery, wet-lab validation, statistical interpretation, and clinical translation. Currently, I am a research assistant in Dr. Yiquan Wang's lab at the University of Florida, where my current question is how antibody frameworks, binding loops, and epitopes shape predicted antigen recognition, and when those predictions can be trusted.

Before this I trained in food science, human nutrition, and the gut microbiome, where I learned many aspects of conducting research: writing grant proposals, developing IRB/GCP-compliant protocols and SOPs, coordinating a randomized crossover clinical trial, processing blood and stool samples at the bench, and interpreting the data with multivariate and regression statistics. I now pair that workflow with AI coding agents such as Claude Code and Codex for antibody design and microbiome analysis.

Long-term Goal

My long-term research interest is to understand why biological exposures and interventions produce different outcomes, identify the interactions and causal mechanisms that drive this variation, and develop interpretable approaches to predict and ultimately modulate these outcomes to improve human health.
