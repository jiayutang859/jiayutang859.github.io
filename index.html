In short

From food and human health to computational antibody design

My path began in food science and clinical nutrition, where I became interested in how biological systems connect diet, microbiome, metabolism, and human health. At the University of Nebraska–Lincoln I helped run a randomized clinical trial and a systematic review on how dietary pulses shape the gut microbiome.

Along the way, generative AI changed how I think about doing science. I'm now building my research direction in computational immunology and antibody engineering, using AI-assisted workflows, structure prediction, and protein design to study molecular recognition. For me, antibodies are a starting point into AI for biology and biomedical design more broadly, not the finish line.

Currently exploring

  • How antibody framework architecture shapes predicted binding when the CDRs, antigen, and epitope are held fixed
  • How much to trust structure-prediction confidence for antibody–antigen complexes
  • What emerging generative design tools such as Germinal and RFantibody can and can't do yet
  • Building reproducible, well-documented pipelines for large-scale computational experiments
  • A longer-term question: how human-health research and AI-driven biomedical design can inform each other
