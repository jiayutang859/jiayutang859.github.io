Current focus

Computational immunology, approached like an experiment

In the Wang Lab I study how antibody frameworks, binding loops, and epitope geometry interact to shape predicted antigen recognition, using structure prediction at scale with native controls and replicate runs, and benchmarking generative antibody design tools against the same controls.

The current project in detail

Three questions I am working on

  • How do framework, CDR, and epitope geometry interact in predicted binding?
  • When is a confident prediction actually a correct one for antibody–antigen complexes?
  • Which generative design outputs are worth experimental testing, and by what criteria?

Trajectory · research vision

The full cycle I am working toward

Not where I have been, but the kind of science I want to be able to do end to end, whatever the system. The centre stays fixed. The cycle keeps turning.

  1. Observe

    Measure real biological systems and phenomena, with controls and provenance.

  2. Understand

    Identify mechanisms, relationships, context, and system-level behaviour.

  3. Predict

    Use quantitative and computational models to anticipate biological behaviour, with uncertainty attached.

  4. Design

    Use those models to intentionally modify or engineer biological interactions.

  5. Validate

    Return predictions and designs to experimental or biological evidence.

  6. Back to observe

    Validation produces new observations, new uncertainty, and new biological questions.

My earlier training sits mostly on the observe and understand side of this loop. My PhD is building depth in predict and design. The long-term goal is to be able to run the whole cycle, and to keep it centred on human biology rather than on any one tool or molecule.

Short bio

Jiayu Tang is a PhD trainee in Dr. Yiquan Wang's lab at the University of Florida, where he is developing expertise in computational immunology, antibody–antigen recognition, structural modeling, and AI-enabled antibody and protein design. His undergraduate and master's research in food science and functional foods, human nutrition, the gut microbiome, systematic review, and a human clinical trial gave him experience across experimental biology, human research, complex systems, and quantitative reasoning. Together, these stages are helping him build toward a research approach that moves across observation, mechanistic understanding, prediction, design, and validation to study human biological interactions.
