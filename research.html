2 · Current research
CurrentOct 2026 – present

How framework context shapes predicted antibody–antigen recognition

Research Assistant · Wang Lab · Dept. of Infectious Diseases & Immunology · University of Florida · with Dr. Yiquan Wang

ObjectiveTo test how the human VH–VL framework, the CDRs, and the epitope geometry interact to shape what structure-prediction and design tools predict about antibody–antigen binding, and to separate a real framework effect from model behaviour.

Tools & methods

  • AlphaFold 3
  • ColabFold
  • RFantibody
  • Germinal
  • AI coding agents (Claude Code, Codex)
  • Python
  • HiPerGator HPC
  • CDR grafting onto alternative frameworks
  • Replicate predictions, reported as distributions
  • Native antibody structures as controls
  • Epitope-masked structural analysis

Metrics: prediction confidence, pose and CDR RMSD, epitope recovery, interface contacts, VH–VL orientation, with replicate spread reported for each.

4 · Trajectory & previous research

Breadth first, then depth

The early years were wide, almost all on the observing and understanding side. My work at UF is adding depth in prediction and design. Each stage below is described by what it taught me, with the projects behind it; the full chronology is in my CV (PDF).

Past · breadth

Observe and understand

  1. Observe

    2022 – 2023
    Northwest A&F University · with Dr. Chunxia Xiao
    • Food science
    • Fermentation
    • Bioactive compounds
    • Assays & controls

    Food science was where I first watched a biological effect exist only next to its control, and disappear when the assay behind it was not understood. It taught me to distrust a number until I knew what the instrument was actually reporting. That habit of careful measurement, and of asking what a measurement can and cannot claim, has followed me into every project since.

    Undergraduate researchApr 2022 – Jun 2023

    Fermentation and functional foods

    Xiao Lab · College of Food Science & Engineering · Northwest A&F University · with Dr. Chunxia Xiao

    In vitro fermentation of proso millet with three lactic acid bacteria, measuring phenolic profile, antioxidant activity, and starch digestibility; spectrophotometric colour analysis of gluten-free millet cakes; contribution to a review on coarse grains and type 2 diabetes. Two co-authored publications in Foods and the Journal of the Science of Food and Agriculture (2023).

    Contributed: measurement discipline; assays, replicates, and controls as the basis of any claimed effect.

  2. Understand

    2023 – 2026
    University of Nebraska–Lincoln · with Dr. Edward Deehan
    • Human nutrition
    • Gut microbiome
    • Clinical trials
    • IRB/GCP protocols
    • Wet lab
    • Systematic review
    • Statistics

    Human nutrition and the gut microbiome showed me biology as a web of interactions: diet meeting microbes, microbes meeting their host, and context deciding what any single intervention does. The same food produced different responders, and reading hundreds of studies side by side made heterogeneity impossible to ignore. I stopped thinking in ingredients and started thinking in interactions, contexts, and the study designs that make an effect interpretable.

    M.Sc. researchJul 2024 – Jul 2026

    Human clinical trial and biobank: dietary pulses, the gut microbiome, and cardiometabolic health

    Deehan Lab · Dept. of Food Science & Technology · University of Nebraska–Lincoln · with Dr. Edward Deehan

    Coordinated a 12-week, three-phase randomized crossover trial on dry bean market classes, the gut microbiome, and cardiometabolic markers (IRB-approved, ClinicalTrials.gov-registered), plus a human biobank study. Co-developed an NIH-style protocol and consent, authored 4 SOPs and 2 clinical workflows, led 200+ clinic visits, and collected and processed microbiome, blood, 24-hour blood pressure, and dietary data, including bench processing of blood and stool samples for biobanking and PCR. Manuscript in preparation; preliminary results presented at the ASM Missouri Valley Branch meeting and Nebraska Research Days. See the CV for presentations.

    Contributed: experimental design, controls, participant variability, wet-lab sample handling, data provenance from consent to analysis, and GCP-level research operations.

    M.Sc. research2024 – 2026

    Systematic review with an LLM agent for data extraction

    Deehan Lab · University of Nebraska–Lincoln

    Led a PICOS-based systematic review of pulse intervention trials across 1,800+ references from three databases. A large-language-model agent we built performed data extraction, and every extracted value was verified by two human reviewers before it was relied on. Gave three guest lectures on search strategy, PICOS design, and AI/ML tools for screening and extraction.

    Contributed: quantitative reasoning about heterogeneity and bias; my first computational method that had to earn trust the way an assay does; habits for documenting and checking automated steps.

Current · depth

Predict and design

  1. Model & predict

    Oct 2026 – now
    Wang Lab · Dept. of Infectious Diseases & Immunology · with Dr. Yiquan Wang
    • Computational immunology
    • Structure prediction
    • Antibody–antigen recognition
    • AI-agent workflows

    Experiments had taught me their own limits: too few people, too few conditions, too little of the design space explored before committing to a measurement. Computation became the way to widen what I could ask, on the condition that a prediction earns trust the way an assay does, with controls, replicates, and provenance. Antibody–antigen recognition is where I am learning that discipline now, because the objects are defined and a prediction can be checked against a known structure.

  2. Modulate & engineer

    developing
    • Antibody engineering
    • Protein design
    • Generative design

    A prediction is only worth something if it can change something. Turning validated factors into a redesigned molecule is the stage I have the least experience in, and the one I am now training toward. It closes the loop: a designed molecule, like a changed diet, becomes a new interaction to observe.
