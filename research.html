1 · Current research
CurrentAug 2026 – present

How framework context shapes predicted antibody–antigen recognition

Wang Lab · Dept. of Infectious Diseases & Immunology · University of Florida · with Dr. Yiquan Wang

The questionWhen the binding loops, antigen, and epitope are held fixed, how do the human VH–VL framework, the CDRs, and the epitope geometry interact to shape what structure-prediction and design tools predict about binding? And how much of any observed effect is biology rather than model behaviour?

Why it matters

Frameworks are often treated as passive scaffolds, but they influence CDR conformation and VH–VL packing. If prediction and design tools are sensitive to framework context, that changes how grafting, humanization, and de novo design should be done, and how far in-silico results can be trusted. Separating a real framework effect from model uncertainty is itself part of the question.

Approach

  • Assemble a provenance-tracked dataset of paired human VH–VL frameworks, parent antibody structures, and epitope definitions
  • Graft fixed CDRs onto alternative frameworks, keeping native antibodies as positive controls
  • Run replicate structure predictions with AlphaFold 3 / ColabFold on HiPerGator and report distributions, not single runs
  • Evaluate with epitope-masked structural analysis
  • Benchmark generative antibody design tools against the same controls

Tools & methods

  • AlphaFold 3
  • ColabFold
  • RFantibody
  • Germinal
  • Python
  • HiPerGator HPC

Metrics: prediction confidence, pose and CDR RMSD, epitope recovery, interface contacts, VH–VL orientation, with replicate spread reported for each.

Current status

In progress. The dataset and evaluation pipeline are built; large-scale predictions and early design-tool benchmarks are under way. Results are preliminary and unpublished.

What I am learning

A confident prediction is not a correct one. The controls and the replicate structure matter as much as the model, and much of the work is deciding what a given metric is entitled to mean. I am also building the structural intuition this field depends on, one structure at a time.

2 · Research questions

What I am working on, and what I am growing toward

The first list is active work. The second is direction, not a set of mature research programs.

Working on now

Active in the Wang Lab.

  • How do framework, CDR, and epitope geometry interact in predicted antibody–antigen binding?
  • When are structure-prediction confidence metrics trustworthy for antibody–antigen complexes, and what controls expose their failure modes?
  • Which generative design outputs are worth experimental testing, and by what criteria?

Growing toward

Directions I want the PhD to open up.

  • How does molecular context shape immune recognition more broadly, beyond antibodies?
  • How can sequence–structure–function relationships guide protein and antibody design under uncertainty?
  • How should experimental controls, replicates, and applicability domains be built into AI-driven biology as standard practice?
  • How can molecular predictions be connected to physiological and translational outcomes, the scale I worked at before?

4 · Research foundations

Previous research, and what each stage contributed

These projects studied different biological questions from my current work. They are here because each one changed how I do research now.

M.Sc. researchJul 2024 – Jul 2026

Human clinical trial and biobank: dietary pulses, the gut microbiome, and cardiometabolic health

Deehan Lab · Dept. of Food Science & Technology · University of Nebraska–Lincoln · with Dr. Edward Deehan

Coordinated a 12-week, three-phase randomized crossover trial on dry bean market classes, the gut microbiome, and cardiometabolic markers (IRB-approved, ClinicalTrials.gov-registered), plus a human biobank study. Co-developed an NIH-style protocol and consent, authored 4 SOPs and 2 clinical workflows, led 200+ clinic visits, and collected and processed microbiome, blood, 24-hour blood pressure, and dietary data. Manuscript in preparation; preliminary results presented at the ASM Missouri Valley Branch meeting and Nebraska Research Days. See presentations.

Contributed: experimental design, controls, participant variability, data provenance from consent to analysis, and GCP-level research operations.

M.Sc. research2024 – 2026

Systematic review with an LLM agent for data extraction

Deehan Lab · University of Nebraska–Lincoln

Led a PICOS-based systematic review of pulse intervention trials. Two reviewers screened 1,800+ references; a large-language-model agent we built performed data extraction, and every extracted value was verified by two human reviewers before it was relied on. Gave three guest lectures on search strategy, PICOS design, and AI/ML tools for screening and extraction.

Contributed: quantitative reasoning about heterogeneity and bias; my first computational method that had to earn trust the way an assay does; habits for documenting and checking automated steps.

Thesis theme2023 – 2026

Gut microbiome and dietary fiber

University of Nebraska–Lincoln

Thesis and trial work on how dietary pulses shape gut microbial communities and host markers, in a system where the same fiber can produce different responders.

Contributed: an interaction-driven, context-dependent view of biological systems that I now apply to framework, CDR, and epitope interactions.

Undergraduate researchApr 2022 – Jun 2023

Fermentation and functional foods

Xiao Lab · College of Food Science & Engineering · Northwest A&F University · with Dr. Chunxia Xiao
